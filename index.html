A1
MSc structural engineering design project · University of Leeds

Vertical Forest, Woodhouse Lane, Leeds

Role
Individual detailed design, after a group concept stage
Level
MSc, 2025–26
Codes
BS EN 1990 to 1995 and 1997, UK National Annexes
Tools
Revit, hand calculations, IStructE Embodied Carbon Calculator

The Woodhouse Lane car park in Leeds sits on a bridge deck over the A58(M) inner ring road. The brief was to replace it with a sustainability-led vertical forest of homes, student rooms and public space, with at least half the building over the existing deck. The precast deck planks cannot take new load, so everything above has to be carried on a new transfer structure.

20storeys, two stepped modular towers
864homes and rooms (240 apartments, 624 student rooms)
145kgCO₂e/m² embodied carbon (A1–A5)
133calculation sheets

Concept choice

Two concepts went through a weighted decision matrix. A steel frame with hollow core floors scored 270 and needed 16,855 kN on the existing bridge columns, well above their 11,287 kN capacity. Lightweight volumetric modules with timber cassette floors scored 355 and kept the column load to 10,977 kN, so no column strengthening was needed for that load.

Structural scheme

  • Off-site modules with RHS corner columns and PFC edge beams in S355, stacked with bolted splices.
  • C24 timber cassette floors at about a third of the weight of a concrete slab.
  • A 2.0 m deep Pratt transfer truss spanning 19.6 m at level 01 onto the bridge columns and new piles.
  • RC cores with perimeter cross-bracing for lateral stability, checked for robustness as Consequence Class 2B.
  • 750 mm CFA piles 15 m deep into sandstone, FRP jacketing and micropile underpinning for the existing structure.

A G+2 steel hostel for an archery academy with a 50 m cantilever shooting shed along one side. Columns were only allowed along the long sides of the building, which pushed the design towards long-span steel beams and profiled deck composite floors.

Hostel50 m × 12.6 m, three storeys
Storey heights3.6 m ground, 3.0 m upper
Archery shed50 m × 7.5 m cantilever, open on three sides
Floors and wallsProfiled deck, gypsum board panels
LoadsDead, live, wind, seismic
Estimate≈ ₹1.37 crore

What I did

  • Drew the plans and sections and set out the column grid.
  • Modelled the frame in STAAD.Pro with load combinations to IS 800:2007, then designed and detailed the critical beams and columns.
  • Checked the critical beam by hand: ISMB 600 over 5 m, plastic section, V = 70 kN against Vd = 944.75 kN, deflection limit L/300 = 16.67 mm.
  • Designed the foundations in STAAD Foundation and built the Revit model.
  • Prepared a detailed Excel estimate covering earthwork, structural steel, deck flooring, drywall and roofing.

B1
MSc dissertation · University of Leeds · Supervisor Dr A. Rahman

Structural fire performance of WAAM stainless steel RHS columns

Role
Independent research
Level
MSc dissertation
Codes
EN 1993-1-4, EN 1993-1-2
Tools
Abaqus/CAE 2023, GMNIA, Static Riks, S4R shells

Wire arc additive manufacturing (WAAM) 3D-prints steel members. Its material properties at high temperature are now published, but nobody had checked how WAAM columns actually buckle in a fire. I asked whether the EN 1993-1-4 column method, calibrated for conventional stainless steel, can be used for WAAM columns without change.

SectionsS1 150×100×5, S2 120×80×5, S3 200×120×5 mm
Column2000 mm, pinned at both ends
Temperatures20, 200, 400, 600, 800 °C
Analyses15 GMNIA runs, 20 mm S4R mesh
MaterialWAAM ER316LSi, two-stage Ramberg-Osgood
Code checkEN 1993-1-4 buckling curve, α = 0.49

What I did

  • Built a two-step GMNIA model: a lateral disturbing force, then Static Riks to peak load.
  • Found and fixed a load introduction error during verification. Added stiffened end zones to S3 to stop local crumpling at the supports.
  • Checked every result against the Euler and squash load bounds.
  • Calculated the matching EN 1993-1-4 resistances with EN 1993-1-2 reduction factors.

B2
BEng final year project · Published in a Scopus-indexed journal

Eccentrically loaded cold-formed steel angle columns with intermediate stiffeners

Role
Team of three, testing and FE modelling
Level
BEng final year
Method
Laboratory tests and FE
Tools
ABAQUS (S4R shells), CUFSM, LVDT

Cold-formed steel angles are light and efficient but sensitive to how the load is applied. We tested angle columns with V, U and Σ shaped intermediate stiffeners under concentric and eccentric compression, then built FE models to extend the results.

  • Tested specimens 250 to 1500 mm long at eccentricities up to 120 mm in a compression testing machine, with an LVDT recording axial shortening.
  • Checked local, distortional and global buckling modes in CUFSM.
  • Built ABAQUS shell models using eigenvalue buckling then Static Riks, with geometric imperfections and material nonlinearity.
  • Validated the model against published tests (test/FE 0.96 to 0.98) and our own tests (mean 0.94, standard deviation 0.027).

Drop in buckling load when the eccentricity acts towards the weaker axis

V stiffener68.9%
Σ stiffener50.4%
U stiffener49.0%

Eccentricity towards the stiffer axis changed the capacity only slightly. The study proposed a design curve for engineering practice and was published in July 2025.

B3
BEng project · Numerical study

Buckling behaviour of perforated thin-walled columns

Role
Team of three, FE modelling
Level
BEng
Variables
Hole shape, opening ratio, spacing ratio
Tools
ABAQUS linear and nonlinear buckling

Openings in a column web save weight and let services pass through, but they cut the buckling capacity. We modelled 250 mm long composite C-section columns with circular and square openings to find which layouts keep the most strength.

  • Varied hole shape, opening ratio D/D0 and spacing ratio S/D0 and compared each case with a column without holes.
  • Ran linear eigenvalue and nonlinear analyses to capture buckling and post-buckling behaviour.
Circular holes9.6% to 24% loss of buckling load
Square holes10.2% to 56.4% loss of buckling load
Best circular layoutD/D0 = 1.5, S/D0 = 1.6
Best square layoutD/D0 = 2, S/D0 = 1.67
